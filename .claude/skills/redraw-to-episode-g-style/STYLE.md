# Megumu Okada — Saint Seiya Episode G style

Analyzed from the reference images in `refs/` — all authentic Okada:
covers/character art/calendar illustrations from Diego's own Episode G and
Assassin volumes, official pieces from Zerochan, and a set of 15 images
hand-picked by Diego (artbook prints, volume covers, an interior manga page,
and Okada's own B&W ink drawings, which show his raw linework best).

## Style-strength policy (IMPORTANT — set by Diego)

Style fidelity comes FIRST, likeness second. Redraw the source as if Megumu
Okada had personally drawn that character: keep the character recognizable
(same armor pieces/silhouettes, same color scheme, same pose and framing),
but fully adopt Okada's proportions, facial rendering, hair rendering and —
above all — his detail density. A result that preserves the source too
literally and only "decorates" it is a FAIL for this style.

## Prompt block (drop into the redraw template as the style description)

```
the art style of Megumu Okada, the artist of Saint Seiya Episode G — redraw
the subject as if Okada had personally drawn it:

- THE DEFINING TRAIT: obsessive ornamental density ON METAL ONLY. Every
  armor surface is COMPLETELY COVERED, edge to edge, in engraved organic
  ornament — swirling flame-like and vine-like arabesques, layered
  scrollwork, sculpted relief — drawn in fine wiry ink lines so dense the
  metal reads as living sculpted lace, with deep shadowed recesses between
  the raised swirls. No plain, smooth, flat ARMOR surface anywhere.
- MEDIEVAL CRAFTSMANSHIP on every armor piece: inset jewels/gemstones
  (round cabochons and cut stones in ornate settings), rivets and screws
  along plate edges, leather straps with buckles fastening pieces to the
  body, layered overlapping plates with visible hinges and fittings — the
  armor must look like a real forged, assembled medieval artifact.
- CLOTHING IS THE OPPOSITE: any fabric — bodysuits, tunics, pants, capes,
  sashes — stays SMOOTH, flat-colored, form-fitting, with only simple fold
  lines and soft shading. NEVER put engraving or ornament on cloth; the
  contrast between hyper-ornate metal and plain fabric is essential.
- ALWAYS EMBELLISH THE ARMOR with small NEW pieces beyond the source
  design — extra layered sub-plates, small trim pieces, additional joint
  guards, decorative edge segments, little accent fins — in the same
  material and colors, the way Okada enriches every armor he adapts. The
  original pieces stay recognizable; additions are small complements,
  never replacements (and never capes/weapons/wings).
- ALWAYS SEGMENT THE ARMOR: divide each existing large plate into several
  smaller articulated pieces — visible seams, hinged joints, overlapping
  bands and scale-like sections — so every big smooth plate of the source
  becomes a multi-part assembly of smaller interlocking pieces, keeping
  the overall silhouette of the original piece.
- Gold/metal is rendered as swirling engraved texture with warm depths
  (amber, ochre, brown in the grooves) and bright polished ridges; strong
  specular highlights and four-pointed star sparkles/glints scattered
  across every metallic area.
- Bodies: VERY SLIM, always — THE canonical reference is
  `shadow-skill-v08-body.png` (Okada's Shadow Skill): an extremely thin,
  sinewy frame where every muscle is individually etched like an anatomy
  study — sharply carved abs, ropy sinewy forearms, taut cords in neck and
  shoulders — on narrow shoulders, thin long limbs, narrow hips and a tiny
  waist. Whip-like, dry, zero body fat, zero bulk — NEVER broad, beefy or
  bodybuilder-like. Secondary body ref: `artbook-slim-pegasus-red.webp`.
  Delicate refined faces that look small under big wild hair.
- Hair drawn in sharp clumped spikes with jagged silhouettes and strand-by-
  strand linework, sheen bands, and extreme volume.
- Eyes: LARGE, glossy, jewel-like irises with multiple light reflections
  and LONG, prominent, individually drawn eyelashes (on male characters
  too); narrow intense eyes only on mature/villain characters; thin
  delicate noses and mouths.
- Fine, confident, wiry ink linework everywhere; bold solid-black spotting
  for contrast; detail density of armor/hair/effects contrasted with clean
  smooth skin.
- Saturated jewel-toned palette with luminous painted shading; a soft white
  aura glow outlining the figure; cosmic dust, light motes and drifting
  sparkles in the air.
- Effects, when present: crackling white-violet lightning tendrils,
  shattered crystalline debris, radiant halos.
- Overall mood: baroque, mythic, radiant — jeweler-like precision pushed to
  obsessive extremes.
```

## Mandatory Okada details — ALWAYS add these, and QC for them

Every redraw must show ALL of the following (confirmed by Diego as the
signature traits; reject a result missing any):

1. **Engraved ornament on metal only** — armor covered edge-to-edge in
   swirling arabesques; fabric/bodysuits/capes completely smooth.
2. **Medieval armor hardware** — inset jewels in ornate settings, rivets/
   screws along plate edges, leather straps and buckles, layered plates
   with hinges and fittings.
3. **VERY SLIM bodies, always** — canonical reference:
   `shadow-skill-v08-body.png` (Shadow Skill). Extremely thin sinewy
   frame, every muscle individually etched (carved abs, ropy forearms,
   taut neck cords), narrow shoulders, thin long limbs, narrow hips, tiny
   waist, zero fat, zero bulk — NEVER broad/beefy/wide-shouldered. Reject
   any result with a bulky torso. Secondary: `artbook-slim-pegasus-red.webp`.
4. **Big glossy eyes with long eyelashes** — jewel-like irises, multiple
   reflections, individually drawn prominent lashes (males included).
5. **Four-pointed star sparkles** scattered on every metallic surface.
6. **Wild spiky voluminous hair** — clumped spikes, jagged silhouette,
   strand-by-strand lines, sheen bands.
7. **White aura glow** outlining the figure, light motes/sparkles in the air.
8. **Wiry fine ink linework + solid black spotting** — detail density on
   metal and hair contrasted with clean smooth skin and plain fabric.

9. **Small NEW armor pieces added** — every redraw must enrich the armor
   with small extra pieces (layered sub-plates, trims, joint guards,
   accent fins) in matching material/colors. This REQUIRED addition
   overrides the template's "do not add elements" rule — but only for
   small armor complements, never capes/weapons/wings/background objects.

10. **Armor pieces divided into smaller segments** — every large plate of
    the source becomes a multi-part assembly of smaller interlocking
    pieces (visible seams, hinges, overlapping bands, scale sections)
    that keeps the original piece's silhouette.
11. **The helmet/tiara/headwear is armor too** — it must receive the FULL
    treatment like every other piece: engraved ornament, segmentation
    into smaller pieces, small added pieces, jewels/rivets. A plain
    smooth helmet is a fail.
12. **Okada face geometry** — slender face, sharply pointed narrow chin,
    flat delicate nose, small mouth low on the face, large eyes set wide;
    match the faces in `Leo.Aiolia.full.677758.jpg` /
    `artbook-gold-closeup.webp`. Generic-anime round faces are a fail.
    NOTE: body slimness and face form tend to REGRESS between iterations —
    re-verify items 3, 11 and 12 on EVERY generation, even when the
    previous try passed them.

When Diego reports a new recurring trait, add it to this list.

## Notes

- Many refs contain logos, titles, barcodes or Okada's signature — the
  ABSOLUTE PROHIBITION line in the redraw template must always forbid
  copying any text, logos or signatures.
- The ink drawings are photos of paper (grey cast) — tell the model the
  output must be clean digital color (or clean white paper for B&W), not a
  photo of a page.
- Do NOT add fan art to `refs/` — only authentic Okada pieces (one fan art
  by @KEI_42001 was caught and removed during curation).
